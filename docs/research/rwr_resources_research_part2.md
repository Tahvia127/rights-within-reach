## Veteran resources

Many homeless veterans, veteran homeowners needing repairs, and veterans facing benefits denials live in Chicagoland. Worth a dedicated section.

**Veterans Assistance Commission of Cook County (VACCC)**
- Web: vaccookcounty.org
- Address: HHC 1100 S. Hamilton C-O11 (Lower Level), Chicago 60612
- Hours: Mon to Fri 8:30 to 3:00
- Established under Military Veterans Assistance Act (330 ILCS 45)
- Emergency Financial Services: rent, mortgage, utilities, food, transportation, hygiene, burial/cremation expenses
- Once every 6 months or once per year depending on service
- Free Veterans Service Officers (VSO) help file VA claims, appeals, survivor benefits

**Cook County Department of Veterans Affairs (CCVA)**
- Web: cookcountyil.gov/agency/veterans-affairs
- Established December 2012
- Network of support services, educational workshops, newsletters

**Illinois Department of Veterans' Affairs (IDVA)**
- Web: veterans.illinois.gov
- Cook County office phone: 312-564-2322 (Wednesdays 8:30 to 4:00)
- Multiple Veteran Service Offices statewide
- Maintains DD-214 records for many Illinois veterans
- Five state-run Veterans' Homes for long-term care
- Veterans' Cash lottery funds grants for PTSD, homelessness, healthcare, disability

**Illinois Armed Forces Legal Aid Network**
- Free legal help for veterans on a wide variety of issues
- Connect through IDVA

**Illinois Warrior Assistance Program**
- 24-hour toll-free: 1-866-554-IWAP (4927)
- PTSD and traumatic brain injury support
- Mandatory TBI screening for returning IL Army National Guard
- Web: illinoiswarrior.com

**Greater Chicago Veterans Food Pantry**
- Address: Hines VA Hospital, 5000 S. 5th Ave., Hines IL, Building 9 Auditorium Lobby
- Hours: Thursdays 10:00 to 12:00
- VA ID card required
- Operated by Greater Chicago Food Depository, 773-247-3663

**RTA Military Service Fare Program**
- Free or reduced fares for service members and veterans
- Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry, Will Counties

### Federal veterans benefits relevant to RWR topics

- **HISA (Home Improvement and Structural Alterations)** for veterans hospitalized at VA in past year, medically necessary accessibility mods
- **VA Healthcare**: see "Apply for VA Healthcare" via VACCC
- **GI Bill education benefits**: VR&E for vocational rehab and employment
- **Veterans Care (state)**: affordable health care for veterans not covered by federal VA

**Find a counselor**
- HUD search tool: apps.hud.gov/offices/hsg/sfh/hcc/fc/

## Worker rights and wage theft

Not in the original 4 RWR topics, but tightly connected to Money & Debt (the debt cascade often starts with wage theft or job loss) and Public Benefits (low-wage workers depend on SNAP/Medicaid).

**Chicago Office of Labor Standards (OLS)**
- Chicago.gov/LaborStandards
- File complaints: 311 or CHI311 app
- Enforces Chicago Minimum Wage, Paid Leave, Paid Sick Leave, Fair Workweek, Anti-Wage Theft ordinances
- 2026 minimum wage: $17.05 (4+ employees), $12.96 (tipped) effective July 1, 2026

**Illinois Department of Labor (IDOL)**
- Web: illinois.gov/agencies/idol
- Online Wage Claim Form
- Enforces state labor and employment laws

**Arise Chicago**
- Web: arisechicago.org

- Founded the campaign for Chicago Office of Labor Standards
- Domestic Workers Bill of Rights advocacy

**Latino Union of Chicago**

- Phone: 312-491-9044
- WhatsApp: 312-492-4574
- Day laborers, household workers, contracted workers
- Free education, advocacy, organizing

**Raise the Floor Alliance**
- Coalition of worker centers in Chicago
- Wage theft, retaliation, discrimination, health and safety

**AFIRE (Alliance of Filipinos for Immigrant Rights and Empowerment)**
- Worker center for Filipino immigrants
- Wage theft and labor rights

**Chicago Workers' Collaborative**

**Legal Aid Chicago Employment Practice**

## Notes for the project

A few more patterns from this expanded research:

**Worker rights are a missing fifth pillar.** Wage theft, unfair scheduling, and lack of paid leave are huge drivers of debt and housing instability. The Chicago Office of Labor Standards, Arise Chicago, and Latino Union do similar work to what RWR does for tenants. Consider whether to expand scope, or at least include warm referrals from the Money & Debt topic.

**Neighborhood orgs are underused.** LUCHA, Bickerdike, BPNC, and dozens more know their service area better than any citywide org. The chatbot could ask "what neighborhood are you in?" and route to a hyperlocal partner when one exists.

**Housing counseling is its own service.** HUD-certified counselors are different from legal aid. They handle homebuyer education, foreclosure mediation, financial coaching. Some users may need a counselor more than a lawyer. The chatbot should distinguish.

**Pro se court help is a separate need.** Many users will go to court without a lawyer. Illinois Court Help, Free Legal Answers, and the CARPLS self-help centers all exist for them. This is a gap the current RWR design does not address.

**LGBTQ+ users have specific needs.** Discrimination in housing and benefits remains common despite the law. Howard Brown, Center on Halsted, Lambda Legal, and Brave Space Alliance should be in the resource directory.

**Domestic violence intersects with everything.** The Safe Homes Act, utility deposit deferrals, Medicaid presumptive eligibility, and special eviction protections all flow from DV status. The chatbot should screen sensitively for DV without forcing disclosure.

**Veterans have a separate parallel system.** VACCC, CCVA, IDVA, the VA itself, and HISA make veteran eligibility a parallel track. If a user is a veteran, they should be routed there first.

**Statute amendments are happening rapidly in 2026.** The Safer Homes Act (Jan 1, 2026), Collection Agency Act update (Jan 1, 2026), new utility Low Income Discounts (Jan and June 2026), SNAP work requirements (Feb 1, 2026), Medicaid renewal changes (Dec 2026), are all hitting in the same year. The RAG should be re-indexed quarterly.
